The Economic Framework

WHY AI EXPANDS THE PROFESSION — NOT SHRINKS IT

Every major AI transformation in professional services has followed the same pattern: AI handles high-volume, low-judgment tasks — freeing professionals for higher-value, higher-volume work. In legal aid contexts, demand is essentially unlimited. There are 388,000 Australians turned away from legal help every year. AI doesn't take lawyer jobs. It creates the capacity to finally serve that unmet demand.

The AI Accountability in Justice Act 2026 mandates human-in-the-loop for all rights-affecting decisions — meaning every AI-assisted legal process requires a qualified lawyer. The Act creates jobs by law, not just by economic logic.

An Invitation to the Profession

JOIN THE PILOT. SHAPE THE FUTURE.

Fair Go Justice is inviting legal professional bodies, law firms, and legal aid commissions to co-design the national pilot program. The pilot — proposed to commence in Queensland and NSW in Q4 2026 — needs legal profession partners to:

  • Co-design the AI-assisted triage model — ensuring it meets professional obligations and client outcomes
  • Train and place the first cohort of AI-Assisted Client Advocates — working within existing Legal Aid structures
  • Evaluate outcomes independently — client satisfaction, access expansion, accuracy of AI-assisted research
  • Report to Parliament — building the evidence base for national legislative reform

This is not a threat to the legal profession. It is the biggest opportunity in a generation to expand access, create careers, and demonstrate that lawyers — not algorithms — are the cornerstone of Australian justice.
