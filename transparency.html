AI-Assisted Research — What That Means

HOW WE USE ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE

Fair Go Justice uses AI-assisted research methodology as a research and synthesis tool — not as a source of truth. Every AI-assisted output is subject to the following eight-rule court-safe filter before publication:

01
Source Verification
Every statistic must trace to a government report, peer-reviewed research, or primary court record. AI does not create statistics — it finds and synthesises them.
02
No Real Names
No real individuals are named in any AI-generated content. All accounts use role-based descriptions only (e.g. "a self-represented litigant", "a public sector worker").
03
Documented Patterns
Illustrative accounts on this site are documented systemic patterns — not verbatim testimonies. They are clearly labelled as such on every page they appear.
04
No Findings of Fact
This site does not make findings of fact about any specific case. It documents systemic patterns and publicly available data.
05
No Legal Advice
Nothing on this site constitutes legal advice. Content is educational, advocacy-based, and documentary in nature.
06
Systemic Framing
All analysis refers to systemic failures — not individual wrongdoing — unless supported by verifiable public record (e.g. royal commission findings, court judgments).
07
Citation on Request
Full source citations for any statistic or data point are available on request at fairgojustice@gmail.com. We publish what we can verify.
08
No Raw AI Output
No AI output is published without human review, editing, and verification against our methodology. AI assists — humans decide what is published.
Community Stories — Methodology

DOCUMENTED PATTERNS vs REAL STORIES

The accounts on our Stories page are labelled clearly as "Documented Patterns / Illustrative Accounts". They are:

  • Drawn from publicly documented systemic failures (royal commissions, court data, Legal Aid reports, NACC findings)
  • Written using AI-assisted research methodology, then reviewed against the eight-rule filter
  • Representative of real patterns — not invented scenarios
  • Not attributable to any specific individual

We are actively building a Verified Community Stories section. Real personal accounts submitted via our story form will be reviewed, consent-confirmed, and published separately with full transparency about their verified status. If you have a real story to share, submit it here →

Movement Supporters — Methodology

PETITION & SUPPORTER NUMBERS

The "Movement Supporters" figure reflects the number of individuals who have engaged with this campaign through petition registration, email sign-ups, social media engagement, and direct outreach responses.

We are building toward a verified digital signature system with plans to integrate with government-recognised identity verification. Until that system is operational, movement supporters should be understood as campaign reach — not a formally verified petition count.

We do not inflate, fabricate, or reset movement supporter numbers. Numbers will only increase as real engagement grows.

Statistics — Source List

WHERE OUR DATA COMES FROM

Every statistic published on this site is sourced from one of the following:

  • Australian Government reports — Robodebt Royal Commission, NACC Annual Reports, Legal Aid Commission data, ABS justice statistics
  • Federal and Supreme Court judgments — publicly accessible via AustLII and court websites
  • Academic research — UNSW Law, University of Melbourne, ANU Centre for AI and Digital Ethics
  • Parliamentary records — Hansard, Senate committee reports, joint committee findings
  • OECD and international bodies — OECD Justice Indicators, UNESCO AI Ethics framework, EU AI Act documentation
  • Major Australian journalism — ABC Investigations, Guardian Australia, Sydney Morning Herald verified investigative reports

Key sourced statistics include: SRL rate tripled 4.4%→12.8% (Federal Circuit Court statistical report 2024); 73+ AI fake citations (UNSW Law, April 2026); Robodebt $548.5M settlement (Royal Commission into Robodebt, 2023); NLAP expired 30 June 2025, 388,000 CLC turn-aways (National Association of Community Legal Centres); OECD judicial integrity score 26% vs 66% average (OECD Justice at a Glance 2024).
