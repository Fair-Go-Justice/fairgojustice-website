WHAT HAPPENS WHEN THE SYSTEM CLOSES EVERY DOOR

A real account of one Australian's seven-year journey through a system designed to help — that didn't.

Location: Sydney, NSW
Duration: Seven years
Agencies engaged: Eight
Outcome: Not one took full responsibility
Where It All Started

19 JULY 2019

A truck rolls over inside the M5 tunnel in Arncliffe, Sydney. The driver — an ordinary Australian working a job — is injured, shaken, and immediately caught up in something much bigger than a road accident.

What follows is not just one person's story. It is a story about what happens in Australia when you don't have money, when the people who are supposed to help don't, and when every door you knock on turns out to be a wall.

Access to Justice

NO LAWYER. NO CHANCE.

After the crash, the driver discovers he has been listed on the insurance policy as a subcontractor — not an employee — without his knowledge. This matters enormously because it means the insurance company can pursue him personally for the cost of the truck. Over $100,000. He had no idea this was happening until the legal demands started arriving.

“I was the one injured. I was the one without information. But because I couldn't afford a lawyer, I had no way to fight it properly. The system doesn't tell you what your rights are. It assumes you already know.”

This is the presumption of innocence in practice. In theory, the driver is innocent until proven guilty. In practice, without a lawyer, he has almost no ability to challenge the evidence against him, understand the proceedings, or navigate a system that runs on professional expertise he can't access.

The Agencies That Were Supposed to Help

EIGHT AGENCIES. SEVEN YEARS.

Australia has built a whole network of agencies to help people when things go wrong. AFCA handles financial complaints. ICAC investigates corruption. LECC oversees police conduct. Fair Trading handles consumer issues. The ATO handles employment disputes. Every single one was engaged. Here is what happened:

AgencyWhat Happened
AFCAClosed the complaint — “outside jurisdiction” because a court had previously dealt with part of the matter.
ICACAcknowledged the complaint, asked for more information, then said NSW Police was “outside” its remit.
LECCTook 44 days to respond. The investigating officer dismissed evidence of police conduct issues.
NSW Fair TradingAcknowledged complaints promptly. Referred to Legal Aid. Legal Aid couldn't help. Back to square one.
ATOAcknowledged the fraudulent employment registration. Referred to Fair Work Australia.
Transport for NSWAcknowledged the vehicle complaint. Still ongoing after months.
NSW PoliceConduct complaint lodged. Outcome issued but findings not disclosed.
Revenue NSWNo complaint pathway available. Debt enforcement continued throughout all other proceedings.

Body-worn video from the incident was requested under GIPA. The internal review described the wrong incident entirely — a different case, a different event. The footage relating to the heavy vehicle rollover on 19 July 2019 remains withheld. That is not an administrative error. That is a system that cannot be trusted to review its own decisions.

Analysis

THE PATTERN THAT CAN'T BE COINCIDENCE

71.4%Agencies using substantially identical template language to decline complaints
8Agencies engaged over seven years
0Agencies that took full responsibility

When responses from all these agencies were analysed, 71.4% used substantially identical template language to decline or avoid complaints — even though these agencies have no documented communication with each other. The same phrases. The same jurisdictional avoidance framing. The same timing patterns.

“These agencies don't talk to each other. But somehow they all speak the same language when they're saying no. That's not a coincidence. That's a system.”

No single agency did anything obviously wrong on its own terms. Each one followed its own rules. But the collective result — eight agencies, seven years, not one that took full responsibility — is a system that has completely failed the person it was meant to serve.

Every Australian

WHY THIS MATTERS FOR EVERY AUSTRALIAN

This case is extreme in its complexity. Most Australians won't face eight agencies simultaneously. But millions of Australians face versions of this story every year:

  • The person who can't afford to challenge a fine they didn't deserve, so they pay it.
  • The tenant evicted without proper notice who couldn't navigate the tribunal system alone.
  • The small business owner who lost everything in a contract dispute they couldn't afford to fight.
  • The worker whose employer changed their employment records and they had no way to prove it.
  • The person wrongly charged with an offence who pleaded guilty because a trial was too expensive and too frightening to face alone.

“The system says ‘innocent until proven guilty.’ The system doesn't say ‘we'll help you prove it.’”

A New Layer of Disadvantage

THE ADVANCED INTELLIGENCE ACCOUNTABILITY GAP

A person in this situation today — navigating nine agencies, multiple courts, over seven years — would turn to Advanced Intelligence tools for help. Courts now issue mandatory Advanced Intelligence governance frameworks. The Family Court Practice Direction (29 May 2026) makes every court user personally responsible for Advanced Intelligence outputs.

Without legal aid to understand those requirements, using Advanced Intelligence to draft an affidavit or prepare submissions creates new liability on top of existing disadvantage. The people with the least access to legal help are the most exposed to the new compliance requirements.

This is one of five systemic failures — and one of five reform pillars — that Fair Go Justice now documents.
